The maths

The surface is two inverted Gaussians and a shallow bowl: f(p) = −Σᵢ Aᵢ exp(−‖p − cᵢ‖² / 2σᵢ²) + 0.08‖p‖², with (A, σ) = (1.00, 0.17) for the sharp well and (0.86, 0.46) for the flat one. The bowl only keeps the trajectories on screen.

SGD: p ← p − η∇f. SGDM: v ← 0.8v − η∇f, p ← p + v. DIESGD, a second-order low-pass on the gradient: s₁ ← s₁ + α(∇f − s₁), s₂ ← s₂ + α(s₁ − s₂), p ← p − ηs₂, with α = 0.16.

MIE keeps three integral orders. Order j is the gradient through j chained leaky integrators: Iⱼ ← (1 − λ)Iⱼ + λIⱼ₋₁, I₀ = ∇f, λ = 0.16. The coefficients descend the hypergradient of the loss with respect to them — ∂L/∂kⱼ = −η ∇fᵀIⱼ — so kⱼ ← kⱼ + 0.15 ∇fᵀIⱼ, clipped to [0.05, 1] and renormalised; the step is p ← p − 0.8 Σ k̂ⱼIⱼ. Step sizes are 0.004, 0.01, 0.03 and 0.8.

"Sharpness" is the trace of the Hessian at the final point, estimated by finite differences. It is not a generalisation bound — just the local curvature that flat-minima work uses as a proxy.

A toy, not an experiment: two wells, hand-tuned constants, no data and no training. The honest parts are the landscape, the update rules and the curvature readout.
