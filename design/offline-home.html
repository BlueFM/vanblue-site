II / About the work

On landscapes & learning.

I study why deep networks generalize and stay robust. My current work asks a narrow version of that question: how the geometry of the loss landscape can be shaped during training, and whether optimizers derived from neurodynamics — integral and inertia terms over the gradient history — can steer a model toward flat minima without extra per-step cost.

Before that I worked on access strategies for mmWave cell-free networks, and on a cascaded inertia SGD whose coefficients are learned offline rather than tuned by hand.

III / Behind the papers

Research Stories

Two projects on one theme: how a second-order integral term filters sharp minima out of the training trajectory, and what changes once the integral order itself becomes adaptive.

Open the loss-landscape workbench →

DIESGD - cascaded inertia optimizer

Standard momentum carries first-order history; it does not care whether the model settles into a sharp or a flat basin.

DIESGD adds a second integral term: the gradient signal is integrated twice before entering the update, which acts as a low-pass filter on the loss surface and removes the sharp components that hurt generalization. The four coefficients weighting these terms are not hand-tuned - a small feed-forward network predicts them, trained offline on noisy gradients through a neurodynamic error equation.

Published as Enhancing Generalisation via Cascaded Inertia SGD With Learnt Hyperparameters.

MIE - meta integration-enhanced optimizer

DIESGD fixes the integral order at two, so it can only filter a limited band of sharp minima - a local view of the optimization problem. The MIE mechanism instead stacks multiple integral orders over the gradient history, each reshaping the loss at its own timescale and generating momentum that does not decay the way EMA momentum does.

The hard part is the coefficients. Their optimal values shift during training, a feed-forward network per order does not scale, and independent integral terms let stale information accumulate. MIE therefore updates the coefficients by a hypergradient rule driven by the directional alignment between the instantaneous gradient and the historical integral states.

Two theoretical results accompany the method: an escape-time approximation showing that SGD with MIE leaves sharp minima faster, and local asymptotic stability of the dynamics via Lyapunov analysis.

Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics - see the paper page.
